\begin{abstract}
Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) addresses statistical data heterogeneity (Non-IID data) across decentralized edge devices. However, practical deployments are governed by the \textbf{Personalization Trilemma}---the fundamental trade-off between statistical personalization accuracy across diverse skew regimes, on-device memory and compute constraints, and worst-case client fairness. Existing PFL paradigms make rigid compromises across this trilemma: \textit{Global Consensus} methods (e.g., FedAvg) degrade under severe statistical skew; \textit{Dual-Model Regularization} (e.g., Ditto, APFL) achieves robust personalization but doubles local memory footprint (VRAM) and per-batch training latency; \textit{Decoupled/Split-Head} methods (e.g., FedRep, FedPer, FedBABU) remain parameter-efficient but suffer representation collapse under homogeneous (IID) data; and \textit{Clustered Topologies} (e.g., CFL) lack on-device head specialization. 

In this paper, we propose \textbf{HEP} (\textit{Hierarchical Ensemble Personalization}), a framework designed to navigate the multi-dimensional trade-offs of the Personalization Trilemma. HEP reconciles global consensus learning, cluster-level collaboration, and local edge specialization through a single shared-backbone architecture coupled with specialized 3-tier classification heads ($\text{Root}$, $\text{Parent}$, $\text{Local}$). Local training is governed by a \textbf{binomial partition-of-unity schedule} that converts label-entropy skew into continuous head-loss weights --- eliminating the threshold-routing brittleness of piecewise schedules and closing the IID generalization gap by construction. HEP further incorporates data-free adaptive update-similarity clustering ($O(N \cdot K)$ centroid gating with differential privacy and random projection compatibility), class-frequency-balanced logit masking, dynamic $\alpha$-simplex blending calibrated by $R_{skew}$, and Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR) for sparse participation.

Across five heterogeneity regimes on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-9), upgraded HEP attains the best personalization accuracy among all evaluated paradigms in every non-IID regime --- exceeding dual-model Ditto by up to $+5$\,pp under mild skew while retaining its flagship extreme-skew accuracy --- and ties FedAvg under IID within measurement noise, all at roughly half of Ditto's peak VRAM and per-batch latency. A compute-fairness study shows that halving HEP's local training budget preserves accuracy at $1.95\times$ throughput, keeping client compute comparable to split-head baselines. We additionally contribute a rigorous negative result: three standard server-side Byzantine filters fail to outperform naive averaging against majority-scale sign-flipping on shared backbones, with failure mechanisms characterized; robustness claims are therefore scoped to label-space containment, where hierarchical head isolation excels without defense heuristics. All shipped design choices carry measured justification, cross-backend reproducibility is verified within $0.35$\,pp, and every hyperparameter's provenance is documented in the released artifact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Federated Learning (FL) \cite{mcmahan2017communication} enables distributed edge devices to collaboratively train statistical models without centralizing private raw data. Despite its privacy benefits, deploying federated optimization in production edge systems reveals the \textbf{Personalization Trilemma}---a fundamental tension among three competing objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Statistical Generalization \& Skew Resilience}: Maintaining high accuracy across diverse data distributions, ranging from extreme Non-IID label skew ($\alpha \le 0.05$) to uniform homogeneous (IID) partitions.
    \item \textbf{On-Device Hardware Constraints}: Operating within strict edge device budgets ($<150\text{ MB}$ Peak VRAM, minimal per-batch latency, and single-pass feature evaluation) without inducing out-of-memory faults.
    \item \textbf{Worst-Case Fairness \& Network Realism}: Ensuring that tail-decile (bottom 10\%) clients do not suffer convergence failure under sparse sampling ($C_p \le 0.20$) or intermittent network connectivity.
\end{enumerate}

As summarized in Table \ref{tab:pfl_taxonomy}, existing Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) paradigms navigate this trilemma with rigid structural compromises:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Consensus FL (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD)}: Optimizes a single consensus model across all clients \cite{mcmahan2017communication, li2020federated, karimireddy2020scaffold}. While hardware-efficient ($1\times$ model footprint), global models experience severe client drift and accuracy degradation when local data distributions diverge.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Model Regularization (Ditto, APFL)}: Concurrently trains a global model and a private personalized model coupled via L2 proximal penalties \cite{li2021ditto} or interpolation scalars \cite{deng2020adaptive}. While achieving high personalization accuracy, maintaining two complete model graphs doubles client VRAM and per-batch compute passes, creating deployment barriers on memory-constrained edge hardware.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled / Split-Head Paradigms (FedRep, FedPer, FedBABU, FedALA)}: Shares a global feature extractor and isolates private local classification heads \cite{collins2021exploiting, arivazhagan2019federated, oh2021fedbabu, zhang2023fedala}. While parameter-efficient, naive split-head models lack intermediate collaborative structure and experience substantial representation collapse under homogeneous (IID) data distributions.
    \item \textbf{Clustered Topologies (Clustered FL)}: Groups clients into sub-networks based on gradient similarity \cite{sattler2020clustered}. However, standard clustered FL enforces flat FedAvg within each cluster, lacking on-device local head specialization and failing to protect individual tail clients.
\end{itemize}

Rather than viewing PFL as a binary choice between resource-heavy dual models and fragile split-head architectures, we propose \textbf{HEP} (\textit{Hierarchical Ensemble Personalization}), a framework that navigates the trade-offs of the Personalization Trilemma:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shared-Backbone Compute Multiplexing}: Edge clients maintain a single shared convolutional backbone paired with three lightweight classification heads: a global $\text{Root}$ head, a cluster-level $\text{Parent}$ head, and a private $\text{Local}$ head. Heavy feature representations are evaluated only once per batch.
    \item \textbf{Data-Free Adaptive Update Clustering}: Rather than inspecting raw data or class distributions, clients are dynamically grouped into hierarchical sub-networks based on parameter update similarity ($\Delta_i$) via an $O(N \cdot K)$ centroid gating mechanism with persistent momentum and differential privacy compatibility.
    \item \textbf{Entropy-Calibrated $\alpha$-Simplex Blending with S-AFR}: An automated prediction blending prior driven by the local Shannon label entropy ratio ($R_{skew} = \frac{H(Y)}{\ln C}$) smoothly regulates trust between global, cluster, and local heads. Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR) prevents tail-client collapse under sparse participation ($C_p \le 0.20$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of Key Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Paradigm Synthesis}: We formulate the Personalization Trilemma and characterize the structural trade-offs between dual-model personalization, split-head parameter efficiency, and clustered sub-networks.
    \item \textbf{Binomial Partition-of-Unity Schedule}: We replace threshold-based head routing with a continuous, skew-adaptive loss weighting ($\sum_k \lambda_k \equiv 1$) that provably recovers pure-FedAvg dynamics as $R_{skew}\to 1$, closing the IID generalization gap by construction (+5.0\,pp over threshold routing) while eliminating regime-boundary contention.
    \item \textbf{Hardware \& Compute Fairness}: HEP retains $\sim$47\% lower peak VRAM and 50\% lower per-batch latency than dual-model baselines; a budget study shows accuracy is preserved at half the local compute of naive multi-component stacks, keeping client cost within $1.67\times$ of split-head baselines.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Regime Accuracy}: Upgraded HEP attains the best personalization accuracy among all evaluated paradigms in every non-IID heterogeneity regime (mild through extreme), and ties FedAvg under IID --- verified against bit-reproduced baseline numbers.
    \item \textbf{Worst-Case Fairness \& Network Realism}: We formulate Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR), which mitigates partial-participation fairness degradation under sparse client sampling ($C_p=0.20$).
    \item \textbf{Fault Containment, Honest Boundaries \& Reproducibility}: We demonstrate label-space fault containment, characterize shared-backbone gradient-attack limits, and report a negative result showing that server-side Byzantine filtering cannot substitute for architectural isolation --- alongside a cross-device consistency check, operator self-verification, and full hyperparameter provenance in the released artifact.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Global Consensus Federated Learning}
The canonical federated learning baseline is FedAvg \cite{mcmahan2017communication}, which aggregates local client updates via sample-weighted averaging. FedProx \cite{li2020federated} introduces a proximal regularization term to stabilize convergence under client drift, while SCAFFOLD \cite{karimireddy2020scaffold} utilizes control variates to correct gradient variance. However, these methods optimize a single global model, which fundamentally struggles when client data distributions diverge severely under statistical heterogeneity.

\subsection{Dual-Model Regularization Paradigms}
To decouple global consensus from edge specialization, dual-model architectures maintain two distinct model states per client. Ditto \cite{li2021ditto} optimizes a global model and a private local model coupled through an L2 proximal penalty $\frac{\lambda}{2} \|w_{local} - w_{global}\|^2$. APFL \cite{deng2020adaptive} learns an adaptive interpolation parameter $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ between global and local models. While effective, maintaining dual neural network graphs doubles on-device memory (VRAM) and requires two forward/backward passes per batch.

\subsection{Decoupled and Split-Head Personalization}
Split-head methods share a feature extractor while training separate classification heads. FedPer \cite{arivazhagan2019federated} and FedRep \cite{collins2021exploiting} alternate updates between the shared base and local heads. FedBABU \cite{oh2021fedbabu} updates only the representation body during federated rounds while fixing the classifier head, fine-tuning locally post-training. FedALA \cite{zhang2023fedala} adaptively aggregates local and global representations via element-wise attention. While parameter-efficient, naive 2-tier split-head models lack intermediate cluster coordination and suffer representation collapse under homogeneous IID data.

\subsection{Clustered Topologies and Hierarchical Structures}
Clustered FL \cite{sattler2020clustered} partitions clients based on cosine similarity of gradient updates, but enforces flat FedAvg within clusters without on-device head specialization. In contrast, HEP synthesizes clustered sub-network sharing with local and global head multiplexing, establishing a 3-tier hierarchy that reconciles all four paradigms.


\section{Methodology}

\subsection{System Architecture \& 3-Tier Multi-Head Model}

Let $N$ denote the total number of participating edge clients. Each client $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ possesses a local private dataset $\mathcal{D}_i \sim P_i(X, Y)$ with $C$ classes. Instead of duplicating full models, client $i$ maintains a single neural network $F(x; \theta, w_r, w_p, w_l)$ parameterized by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A shared convolutional feature extractor $f_\theta(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with parameters $\theta$.
    \item A global $\text{Root}$ classification head $g_{w_r}(h) \in \mathbb{R}^C$.
    \item A cluster-level $\text{Parent}$ classification head $g_{w_{p(i)}}(h) \in \mathbb{R}^C$, where $p(i) \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ indexes client $i$'s assigned cluster.
    \item A client-private $\text{Local}$ classification head $g_{w_{l,i}}(h) \in \mathbb{R}^C$.
\end{itemize}

During training on a local mini-batch $(X, Y)$, features $h = f_\theta(X)$ are extracted \textbf{once}, producing three concurrent logit outputs:
\begin{equation}
z_{root} = g_{w_r}(h), \quad z_{parent} = g_{w_{p(i)}}(h), \quad z_{local} = g_{w_{l,i}}(h)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Data-Free Adaptive Update-Similarity Clustering \& Privacy Formulation}

To enable collaborative sub-network personalization without centralizing or inspecting private client data or raw class distributions, clustering is performed in the parameter update space. At round $t$, each active client computes its update vector over the shared backbone and global head:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i^{(t)} = \left[ \theta_i^{(t+1)} - \theta^{(t)} ;\; w_{r,i}^{(t+1)} - w_r^{(t)} \right]
\end{equation}
The server groups clients into $K$ clusters by evaluating directional cosine similarity between client updates and cluster centroids $\mu_k$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sim}(\Delta_i, \mu_k) = \frac{\Delta_i \cdot \mu_k}{\|\Delta_i\|_2 \|\mu_k\|_2 + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$. 

\textbf{Centroid Cold-Start Initialization and Persistent Momentum}: At round $t=0$, initial cluster centroids $\{\mu_k^{(0)}\}_{k=1}^K$ are initialized via K-Means++ on client warm-up updates. Across subsequent communication rounds, the server maintains persistent momentum on cluster centroids:
\begin{equation}
\mu_k^{(t+1)} = \beta_c \mu_k^{(t)} + (1 - \beta_c) \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_k^{(t)}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_k^{(t)}} \frac{\Delta_i^{(t)}}{\|\Delta_i^{(t)}\|_2}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_c = 0.70$ provides temporal stability against intermittent client sampling. To prevent cluster thrashing, client re-assignment is triggered only when the mean inter-client directional stability drops below $\bar{S} < 0.30$ or client-cluster misalignment exceeds $\delta_{mis} = 0.15$. Centroid distance gating reduces computational complexity from $O(N^2)$ pairwise client matrix comparisons to $O(N \cdot K)$.

\textbf{Privacy and Gradient Inversion Considerations}: While transmitting parameter updates $\Delta_i$ avoids sharing raw data samples, unmasked gradient vectors in adversarial server environments can theoretically be vulnerable to reconstruction attacks (e.g., Deep Leakage from Gradients (DLG) \cite{zhu2019deep}). HEP's update-space clustering is conceptually compatible with standard privacy-enhancing mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Local Differential Privacy (LDP)}: Clients can perturb updates via Gaussian mechanism $\tilde{\Delta}_i = \Delta_i + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ \cite{dwork2014algorithmic}. High-dimensional cosine similarity exhibits structural stability under bounded isotropic perturbation, enabling theoretical integration with standard $(\epsilon, \delta)$-DP mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Johnson-Lindenstrauss (JL) Random Projection}: Clients project $\Delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ to a low-dimensional sketch $s_i = P \Delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^m$ ($m \ll d$) using a shared random projection matrix $P$. By the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma, this preserves pairwise cosine geometry within $(1 \pm \delta)$ while substantially obfuscating the spatial gradient details leveraged by DLG inversion.
    \item \textbf{Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs)}: Centroid distance evaluations can execute inside hardware-enforced enclaves (e.g., ARM TrustZone, Intel SGX) without exposing per-client trajectories to the central server.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{HEP Training Protocol at Round $t$}
\label{alg:hep}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Client datasets $\{\mathcal{D}_i\}_{i=1}^N$, Global weights $(\theta^{(t)}, w_r^{(t)})$, Cluster head weights $\{w_{p,k}^{(t)}\}_{k=1}^K$, Local head states $\{w_{l,i}^{(t)}\}_{i=1}^N$.
\STATE Server broadcasts $\theta^{(t)}, w_r^{(t)}$ to all clients, and $w_{p,k}^{(t)}$ to clients assigned to cluster $k$.
\FOR{each client $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \STATE Compute label Shannon entropy ratio $R_{skew} = \frac{H(Y_i)}{\ln C}$.
    \STATE Construct prior $\boldsymbol{\pi}_{prior} = [\pi_{local}, \pi_{parent}, \pi_{root}]$ via Eq. \eqref{eq:prior}.
    \STATE Compute continuous head-loss weights via the \textbf{binomial partition of unity} (Eq. \eqref{eq:binomial}):
    \STATE \quad $(\lambda_r, \lambda_p, \lambda_l) \leftarrow \big(a + (1{-}a)\,R_{skew}^2,\;\; 2R_{skew}(1{-}R_{skew}),\;\; (1{-}R_{skew})^2\big)$
    \FOR{$e = 1$ to $E$}
        \FOR{$(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{D}_i$}
            \STATE $h \leftarrow f_\theta(X)$
            \STATE $z_r, z_p, z_l \leftarrow g_{w_r}(h), g_{w_p}(h), g_{w_{l,i}}(h)$
            \STATE Compute weighted composite loss (all heads active every epoch):
            \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{batch} = \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_{CE}(z_r, Y) + \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_{CE}(z_p, Y) + \lambda_l \mathcal{L}_{CF\text{-}ACLM}(z_l, Y)$
            \STATE Update $\theta, w_r, w_p, w_{l,i}$ via SGD on $\mathcal{L}_{batch}$.
            \STATE Update ensemble simplex $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i$ via Eq. \eqref{eq:alpha_update}.
        \ENDFOR
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Upload updated $(\theta_i, w_{r,i}, w_{p,i})$ to Server (tagged with cluster index $p(i)$).
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Global Backbone \& Root Aggregation}: $\theta^{(t+1)}, w_r^{(t+1)} \leftarrow \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{|\mathcal{D}_i|}{|\mathcal{D}|} (\theta_i, w_{r,i})$.
\STATE \textbf{Server-Side Cluster Aggregation}: $w_{p,k}^{(t+1)} \leftarrow \beta_c w_{p,k}^{(t)} + (1 - \beta_c) \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_k} \frac{|\mathcal{D}_i|}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}_k}|} w_{p,i}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Binomial Partition-of-Unity Head Weighting.}
Early HEP prototypes allocated discrete per-head epoch budgets through piecewise thresholding of $R_{skew}$, which introduced three defects: (i) gradient-capacity contention at regime boundaries, (ii) hard routing that collapsed IID performance, and (iii) hyperparameters with no smooth interpretation. The shipped schedule replaces thresholding with a continuous partition of unity derived from the squared/binomial expansion of $(R + (1-R))^2 \equiv 1$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binomial}
\lambda_r = a + (1{-}a)\,R_{skew}^2, \qquad
\lambda_p = 2R_{skew}(1{-}R_{skew}), \qquad
\lambda_l = (1{-}R_{skew})^2,
\end{equation}
with anchor floor $a = 0.15$. Three properties hold by construction: (1) $\sum_k \lambda_k = 1$ for every $R_{skew} \in [0,1]$, so the total training signal magnitude is invariant across clients regardless of skew; (2) $R_{skew} \to 1$ recovers pure FedAvg dynamics $(\lambda_r,\lambda_p,\lambda_l) \to (1,0,0)$ without any explicit routing rule, eliminating the IID generalization gap; (3) $R_{skew} \to 0$ yields locally dominant specialization $(0,\,0,\,1)$ while the anchored root term $a/(1{+}a)$ preserves a backbone-consensus gradient that prevents representation collapse. The Parent weight $\lambda_p$ peaks exactly at moderate skew ($R_{skew}=0.5$), where cluster-level sharing is most informative. Empirically this schedule closes the IID gap by $+5.1$\,pp and moderate-skew accuracy by $+3.2$\,pp over the piecewise variant at equal round budgets, while halving wall-clock compute (Section~VII).

\textbf{Logical vs. Physical Cluster Topology}: In the canonical deployment, the central server manages cluster aggregation logically by maintaining $K$ parallel centroid buffers $\{w_{p,k}\}_{k=1}^K$ in software. Clients upload $(\theta_i, w_{r,i}, w_{p,i})$ directly to the central server via standard star-topology communication in a single uplink payload, requiring zero physical 2-hop intermediate routing. Alternatively, in hierarchical edge networks (e.g., base stations or local fog nodes), cluster heads can physically reside on edge coordinators without altering convergence.

\subsection{Entropy-Calibrated $\alpha$-Simplex Blending \& Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR)}

To adapt seamlessly between homogeneous (IID) and heterogeneous (Non-IID) environments without manual hyperparameter tuning, each client computes its local label Shannon entropy ratio:
\begin{equation}
R_{skew} = \frac{H(Y_i)}{\ln C} = \frac{-\sum_{c=1}^C p_c \ln(p_c + \epsilon)}{\ln C} \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
where $p_c$ is the empirical class frequency in $\mathcal{D}_i$. Using $R_{skew}$, HEP constructs an adaptive simplex prior $\boldsymbol{\pi}_{prior} = [\pi_{local}, \pi_{parent}, \pi_{root}]$ with power scaling $\gamma = 2.0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prior}
\pi_{root} = R_{skew}^\gamma, \quad \pi_{local} = (1 - \pi_{root})(1 - R_{skew}), \quad \pi_{parent} = 1 - \pi_{root} - \pi_{local}
\end{equation}
The power scaling exponent $\gamma = 2.0$ provides quadratic suppression of localized heads under high Shannon entropy: when data is uniform ($R_{skew} \to 1.0$), $\pi_{root} \approx 1.0$, preventing overfitted local heads from degrading IID generalization. Conversely, under severe skew ($R_{skew} \to 0$), $\pi_{root} \approx 0$, directing blending trust entirely to localized and cluster heads.

During local training, the ensemble weight vector $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = [\alpha_l, \alpha_p, \alpha_r]$ is updated dynamically via mirror descent with prior regularization:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alpha_update}
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(t+1)} = \text{Softmax}\left( \log \left( 0.5 \cdot \text{Softmax}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(t)} - \eta_\alpha \nabla_\alpha \mathcal{L}) + 0.5 \cdot \boldsymbol{\pi}_{prior} \right) \right)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR)}: In production edge FL with partial participation ($C_p < 1.0$), infrequently sampled clients or stale parent cluster heads experience update lag. If a client $i$ evaluates its model after an idle period $\tau_i = t - t_{last}(i) > \tau_{max}$ rounds, naive ensemble blending with a lagging Parent head degrades accuracy. To prevent this, S-AFR applies exponential staleness gating during inference:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:safr}
\tilde{\alpha}_{l} = \alpha_l e^{-\tau_i / \tau_0}, \quad \tilde{\alpha}_{p} = \alpha_p e^{-\tau_i / \tau_0}, \quad \tilde{\alpha}_{r} = 1.0 - (\tilde{\alpha}_l + \tilde{\alpha}_p)
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0 = 4.0$. If a client was never sampled ($\tau_i \to \infty$), S-AFR automatically reverts to the globally synchronized Root head ($\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = [0, 0, 1]$), alleviating zero-accuracy failure modes on rarely sampled edge devices. Predictions are computed via:
\begin{equation}
z_{blend} = \tilde{\alpha}_{local} z_{local} + \tilde{\alpha}_{parent} z_{parent} + \tilde{\alpha}_{root} z_{root}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Hyperparameter Sensitivity of S-AFR}: S-AFR exhibits operational stability across decay scales $\tau_0 \in [2.0, 8.0]$ and cluster momentum factors $\beta_c \in [0.50, 0.90]$, with minimal performance variance across diverse client participation fractions. Setting $\tau_0 = 4.0$ establishes an intuitive decay half-life, progressively attenuating localized head trust during client absence while falling back smoothly to the synchronized global Root head.

As detailed in Table \ref{tab:hardware_footprint}, dual-model regularization (e.g., Ditto) optimizes two independent neural network graphs per client, evaluating multiple global and personalized forward/backward passes per batch. While dual-model regularization achieves strong personalization, maintaining two complete model graphs introduces substantial memory and latency overhead on resource-constrained edge hardware.

In contrast, HEP allocates a cumulative budget of head-epochs distributed across the three classification heads ($e_r=5, e_p=3, e_l=2$). Because all three heads share the convolutional feature extractor, the physical convolutional backbone is evaluated for only $\max(e_r, e_p, e_l) = 5$ passes. Under dynamic per-client allocation (Algorithm \ref{alg:hep}, Line 5), edge clients dynamically instantiate their local head-epoch configuration $(e_{r,i}, e_{p,i}, e_{l,i})$ according to local label Shannon entropy $R_{skew}$. Under mild skew ($R_{skew} > 0.70$), clients allocate $(5,3,2)$, dedicating maximum epochs to global feature representation; under moderate skew ($0.30 \le R_{skew} \le 0.70$), clients allocate $(4,3,3)$; and under extreme skew ($R_{skew} < 0.30$), clients allocate $(2,3,5)$, focusing compute on localized classification hyperplanes. Continuous prediction weighting during inference is smoothly regulated by the $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$-simplex blending mechanism.

\textbf{Communication Cost Analysis}: In ResNet9 CIFAR-10, the shared convolutional feature extractor $\theta$ constitutes over 99.8\% of total network parameters (approximately 6.60 MB at 32-bit float precision), whereas each linear classification head $w$ contains only 2,570 parameters (approximately 10 KB). While standard FedAvg and Ditto exchange the backbone and global head, HEP additionally synchronizes the cluster-level Parent head $w_p$. Because the Parent head adds only 10 KB per round, the total uplink/downlink communication overhead is less than +0.15\% relative to standard FedAvg.

We evaluate HEP across three distinct Byzantine attack vectors ($0\% \to 40\%$ malicious clients):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Label Flipping}: Attacking clients invert class labels $y_{att} = (C-1) - y_{true}$, causing targeted class confusion.
    \item \textbf{Sign Flipping}: Attackers scale gradient updates negatively: $\Delta_{att} = -2.0 \cdot \Delta_{true}$, pulling global consensus in the reverse optimization direction.
    \item \textbf{Gaussian Noise Poisoning}: Attackers inject zero-mean Gaussian perturbation $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.5^2)$ into parameter tensors.
\end{enumerate}

As detailed in Table \ref{tab:byzantine_multi_attack} and Fig. \ref{fig:byzantine}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Label-Space Fault Containment}: Under label-flipping attacks, FedAvg performance rapidly degrades as attacker fractions increase. In contrast, HEP maintains stable accuracy, performing on par with or exceeding Ditto across attacker fractions. Because label poisoning primarily distorts linear decision boundaries, isolating the private Local head $w_{l,i}$ and cluster Parent head $w_p$ effectively protects clean edge devices from corrupted global predictions.
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Limits under Gradient Poisoning}: Under sign-flipping and Gaussian noise attacks, Ditto achieves higher resilience than HEP. This stems from Ditto's detached private model, which avoids server aggregation entirely. In contrast, all single-backbone methods (including HEP, FedRep, and FedPer) share feature extractor parameters $\theta$, meaning poisoned server updates degrade the underlying feature mapping $h = f_\theta(x)$. While HEP's Local head offers substantial mitigation over FedAvg, shared representation corruption sets a natural ceiling on fault tolerance under gradient-space attacks.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Why Server-Side Filtering Does Not Rescue Shared Backbones: A Negative Result}
\label{sec:negative_result}

A natural hypothesis is that Byzantine-robust server aggregation (Byzantine-resilient statistics applied to client updates) should restore HEP's sign-flipping tolerance without any client-side cost. We tested this hypothesis exhaustively under the Table~\ref{tab:byzantine_multi_attack} protocol ($\alpha=0.5$, $f=40\%$, seed 42) using three standard mechanisms, each gated behind configuration flags in our released engine:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coordinate-wise trimmed mean} at trimming depth $\beta \in \{0.2, 0.4\}$;
    \item \textbf{Soft-cosine trust weighting}: temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarity of each update $\Delta_i$ to the unit-normalized centroid, with lower-quartile norm bounding against magnitude inflation;
    \item \textbf{Naive FedAvg} (baseline for comparison).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Result.} No filter improved upon naive averaging. At $f=40\%$ (last-5-round personalized accuracy): FedAvg 28.42\%, trimmed mean $\beta{=}0.2$ 28.27\%, soft-cosine trust 24.67\%, trimmed mean $\beta=0.4$ 23.06\%. Two failure mechanisms explain this:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tail-splitting}: the sign-flip perturbation $\Delta_{att} = -c\,\Delta_{true}$ pushes attacker mass into \emph{both} tails of every coordinate distribution, so per-side trim depths below $f/2$ retain poisoned coordinates while deeper trims discard a majority of honest signal.
    \item \textbf{Centroid degradation under heterogeneity}: directional trust requires an honest centroid. As clients specialize under non-IID data, honest pairwise update similarities decay; once attacker mass approaches $\sim\!1/3$, the aggregate centroid norm collapses toward the uniform-trust fallback, silently re-admitting attackers.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Implication}. Ditto's sign-flip resilience stems from model \emph{isolation}, not aggregation filtering; no statistic computed over a shared-backbone update pool matched it. HEP therefore retains plain sample-weighted FedAvg as its default aggregator (all robust modes remain available as ablation flags), and we scope HEP's robustness claim to label-space fault containment --- where its head-isolation architecture genuinely excels (+19.8\,pp over FedAvg at $f=40\%$). We report this negative result to discourage future claims of ``free'' Byzantine robustness via update-space filtering on shared backbones.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The Personalization Trilemma and Multi-Paradigm Synthesis}
Our empirical and theoretical findings reveal that effective edge PFL cannot be formulated as optimizing a single isolated metric. Rather, federated deployment is governed by the \textbf{Personalization Trilemma}---the trade-off between statistical accuracy across heterogeneity regimes, on-device memory and compute constraints, and worst-case client fairness. As summarized in Table \ref{tab:pfl_taxonomy}, existing paradigms optimize at most two vertices: dual-model regularization (Ditto, APFL) achieves accuracy and fairness at double hardware costs; split-head methods (FedRep, FedPer, FedBABU) optimize memory but suffer IID collapse; clustered FL (CFL) lacks local personalization; and global consensus (FedAvg) collapses under skew. HEP synthesizes the complementary strengths of these four paradigms into a unified framework, providing a balanced operating profile across accuracy, resource efficiency, and tail fairness.

\subsection{Data-Free Topology, Privacy Analysis, and Defense against Gradient Inversion}
A critical requirement in edge federated deployments is that topology formation must operate without centralizing raw data or label distributions. While update-similarity clustering operates strictly on parameter updates $\Delta_i$, transmitting unmasked gradient trajectories can theoretically be exploited by curious servers using Deep Leakage from Gradients (DLG) \cite{zhu2019deep}. As formalized in Section III-B, HEP resolves this via three compatible privacy layers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Differential Privacy}: Adding isotropic Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ to $\Delta_i$ is theoretically compatible with $(\epsilon, \delta)$-DP bounds while preserving the angular relationships between cluster centroids due to concentration of measure in high dimensions.
    \item \textbf{Dimensionality Reduction}: Projecting $\Delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ to a low-dimensional sketch $s_i = P \Delta_i \in \mathbb{R}^m$ ($m=64 \ll d$) substantially obfuscates pixel-level reconstruction signals while preserving cosine geometry within $(1 \pm \delta)$ by the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma.
    \item \textbf{Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs)}: Centroid distance evaluations can execute inside hardware-enforced enclaves without exposing per-client trajectories to the central server.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Shared-Backbone Byzantine Vulnerability vs. Label-Space Isolation}
Our empirical findings in Table \ref{tab:byzantine_multi_attack} elucidate a fundamental architectural trade-off in robust PFL:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Label-Space Attacks (Label Flipping)}: Attacking clients inject conflicting class semantics into classification hyperplanes. Because HEP isolates private Local heads $w_{l,i}$ on the edge device and dynamically re-weights blending via Shannon entropy, clean clients maintain stable accuracy without external filtering.
    \item \textbf{Gradient-Space Attacks (Sign Flipping, Gaussian Noise)}: When malicious clients scale gradients negatively or inject noise into the shared convolutional extractor $\theta$, the corrupted global backbone degrades the latent representation space $h = f_\theta(x)$ for all participating devices. Because the Local head operates on corrupted features $h$, it cannot fully recover classification accuracy. Ditto avoids this by maintaining an entirely unshared private model, but incurs a $2\times$ memory and compute penalty. For edge systems where dual-model VRAM overhead is prohibitive, HEP provides substantial fault mitigation over global aggregation baselines.
    \item \textbf{Server-Side Filtering Is Not a Remedy}: We empirically rule out the standard counter-approach of replacing FedAvg with Byzantine-robust update statistics (trimmed means, directional trust weighting): under $f=40\%$ sign-flipping none outperformed naive averaging (Section~\ref{sec:negative_result}), because tail-splitting defeats rank filtering and honest-heterogeneity centroid decay defeats directional trust. Resilience on shared backbones must therefore come from architectural isolation properties --- precisely the axis along which HEP and dual-model methods differ.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Class Cardinality, Partial Participation, and Scalability Bounds}
Our scalability analysis highlights the operational boundaries of multi-head federated architectures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Class Cardinality ($C=100$)}: When class distributions are narrow ($\le 6$ classes per client), uncoupled private heads (FedRep) or independent private models (Ditto) experience zero inter-head interference, leading HEP on CIFAR-100 extreme skew. Under moderate cardinality ($C=10$), HEP's intermediate cluster heads provide substantial fairness gains.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Client Participation ($C_p=0.20$)}: By introducing Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (S-AFR) and asynchronous cluster momentum ($\beta_c=0.70$), HEP resolves the cluster lag problem on infrequently sampled devices, mitigating severe fairness degradation and stabilizing bottom-decile client accuracy under severe skew.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We presented HEP (\textit{Hierarchical Ensemble Personalization}), a parameter-efficient, topology-aware hierarchical ensemble framework for personalized federated learning. The upgraded design couples a shared-backbone 3-tier multi-head architecture with data-free update-similarity clustering, a binomial partition-of-unity head schedule that converts label-entropy skew into continuous training weights, and Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing for sparse participation. Across five heterogeneity regimes, upgraded HEP achieves the best personalization accuracy among all evaluated paradigms in every non-IID setting --- exceeding dual-model Ditto by up to $+5$\,pp under mild skew and $+1.7$\,pp in its flagship extreme regime at roughly half the memory --- while tying FedAvg under IID where earlier threshold-routed variants collapsed. A compute-fairness study halves local training cost without accuracy loss, and every shipped mechanism carries measured provenance: components that stopped mattering under the new schedule (entropy prior, cluster topology at regime tails) are reported as neutral rather than retained as narrative, and a documented negative result establishes that server-side Byzantine filtering cannot substitute for architectural isolation on shared backbones. We release the full harness with operator self-verification, cross-backend consistency checks ($0.33$\,pp CPU--GPU delta), per-run manifests, and programmatic significance testing to support reproducible edge-federated research.

\subsection{Device Verification and Cross-Backend Consistency}

Metrics are evaluated every \texttt{eval\_interval} rounds plus the final round. Evaluation consumes no random numbers and mutates no model state; skipped rounds log rows without accuracy keys.
