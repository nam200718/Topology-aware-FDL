\begin{abstract}
Federated Learning (FL) on resource-constrained edge devices faces conflicting demands: mitigating client drift under non-IID data, achieving personalization without dual-model memory overhead, and resisting Byzantine attacks without penalizing honest specialized clients. We introduce \textbf{\fedhep{}} (\textit{Federated Hierarchical Ensemble Personalization}), a lightweight framework that coordinates three representation tiers---global consensus, collaborative peer clusters, and private local heads---over a single shared backbone. \fedhep{} incorporates Active-Class Logit Masking (\textbf{ACLM}) to eliminate gradient interference from unobserved categories, low-dimensional Random Projections (\textbf{RP}) for privacy-preserving clustering, and subspace-constrained update verification. Extensive empirical evaluations across heterogeneous vision benchmarks and adversarial settings demonstrate that \fedhep{} consistently achieves superior personalization accuracy and tail fairness, robust Byzantine resilience, and minimal computational and memory overhead on edge devices.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Federated Learning, Multi-Scale Personalization, Byzantine Fault Tolerance, Statistical Heterogeneity, Tail Fairness, Edge Computing}
