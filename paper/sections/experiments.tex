\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

We evaluate \fedhep{} on fine-grained vision benchmarks (Federated Extended MNIST [FEMNIST]~\cite{caldas2018leaf} and CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}) partitioned under an edge-budgeted subsampling protocol to mirror on-device sample scarcity. Table~\ref{tab:exp_setup} summarizes benchmark settings, Dirichlet heterogeneity regimes~\cite{hsu2019measuring}, neural backbones (ResNet-9~\cite{he2016deep} and MobileNetV3-Small~\cite{howard2019searching}), threat models~\cite{fang2020local,bhagoji2019analyzing,shejwalkar2021manipulating}, and edge training configurations.

We compare \fedhep{} against six representative baselines spanning three paradigms:
(1)~\textit{Global Consensus FL}: FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}, FedProx~\cite{li2020federated} (proximal weight $\mu=0.01$), and SCAFFOLD~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold};
(2)~\textit{Personalized FL}: Ditto~\cite{li2021ditto} (personalization penalty $\lambda=0.05$) and FedRep~\cite{collins2021exploiting}; and
(3)~\textit{Byzantine-Robust Aggregation}: Multi-Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine}.
Our threat models cover four canonical attack vectors~\cite{fang2020local,bhagoji2019analyzing,shejwalkar2021manipulating}: Label Flipping, Sign Flipping, Gradient Ascent, and Gaussian Noise Injection, across attacker ratios $q \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$.

\input{figures/table0_setup}

All benchmark runs report mean and standard deviation across three random seeds ($\{42, 123, 7\}$). Performance is evaluated via Local Test Accuracy (mean Top-1 accuracy evaluated on each client's private test partition), while worst-decile tail fairness is quantified via the Bottom-10\% metric~\cite{li2020fair} (mean accuracy of the lowest-performing decile of edge nodes). The complete implementation and reproduction scripts are provided in the supplementary material (and will be released publicly upon publication).

\paragraph{Personalization Performance under Statistical Heterogeneity}
Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark} and Figures~\ref{fig:femnist_personalization}--\ref{fig:cifar100_personalization} report test accuracy and tail fairness on FEMNIST and CIFAR-100 across five Dirichlet skew regimes, from balanced IID ($\alpha=\infty$) to extreme non-IID ($\alpha=0.05$). In benign IID settings ($\alpha=\infty$), global consensus models (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD) achieve solid accuracy ($\approx 84\%$ on FEMNIST, $\approx 41\%$ on CIFAR-100), but degrade steadily as skew intensifies. In contrast, on FEMNIST, \fedhep{} achieves $94.73\%$ accuracy and $83.91\%$ bottom-10\% fairness under extreme skew ($\alpha=0.05$), outperforming FedAvg by $+16.52$ percentage points (pp) in accuracy and $+38.89$\,pp in tail fairness, while matching the accuracy of dual-model Ditto ($94.93\%$) without duplicating feature backbones.

On the 100-class CIFAR-100 benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark}B and Figure~\ref{fig:cifar100_personalization}), standard global consensus degrades under extreme skew: FedAvg drops to $36.29\%$ with tail fairness falling to $24.87\%$. While split-head FedRep adapts well under extreme skew ($60.97\%$), it experiences severe performance degradation on homogeneous IID data ($16.08\%$, a $25.38$\,pp drop below FedAvg), because isolated heads overfit on limited local samples without global classifier pooling. In contrast, \fedhep{} achieves higher accuracy under extreme skew (\textbf{65.49\%}, outperforming dual-model Ditto by $+5.81$\,pp and FedRep by $+4.52$\,pp) with the highest worst-decile fairness (\textbf{53.92\%}), while avoiding split-head degradation on balanced IID data ($40.94\%$). Under moderate skew ($\alpha=0.5$), \fedhep{} achieves \textbf{46.36\%} accuracy and $39.86\%$ tail fairness, consistently surpassing Ditto ($43.84\%$ / $37.55\%$) and FedAvg ($39.80\%$ / $35.19\%$).

\paragraph{Byzantine Multi-Attack Robustness under Heterogeneous Skew}
Table~\ref{tab:cifar100_byzantine} and Figure~\ref{fig:byzantine_robustness} evaluate defense resilience under four coordinated adversarial vectors on CIFAR-100: Label Flipping, Sign Flipping, Gradient Ascent, and Gaussian Noise Injection, across attacker fractions $q \in \{0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$. A key empirical finding is the failure mode of classical Euclidean aggregation under non-IID data: Multi-Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine} degrades even in benign settings without attackers ($q=0.0$, achieving only $25.04\%$), because legitimate label divergence is conflated with poisoning. By evaluating update alignment within observed label support (\sccf{}), bounding backbone gradient norms, and tracking temporal trust (\ttt{}), \fedhep{} sustains high accuracy in benign environments (\textbf{47.88\%}) and maintains robust performance up to $q=0.30$. Under Gaussian noise ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} maintains \textbf{44.53\%} accuracy (a drop of only $-3.35$\,pp), whereas FedAvg drops to $1.30\%$ and Multi-Krum achieves $22.96\%$. Under gradient ascent ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} sustains \textbf{28.93\%} compared to $1.81\%$ for Ditto and $1.06\%$ for FedAvg.

\paragraph{Multiagent Scalability and Tail Fairness (50 Clients)}
To evaluate behavior in larger multiagent edge populations, we scale the network to $N=50$ clients with 20\% partial client participation per round ($C_p=0.20$, Table~\ref{tab:scale_50clients} and Figure~\ref{fig:scalability_50clients}). Under partial participation, clients unselected over multiple rounds become stale. \fedhep{} incorporates Staleness-Aware Fallback Routing (\textbf{S-AFR}): when evaluating between active rounds, if a client's local head has become stale (unselected for $\Delta t_i$ rounds), inference weights smoothly shift from the stale Local head ($w_l$) toward the aggregated Root ($w_r$) and Parent ($w_p$) models, preventing outdated local parameters from degrading local test accuracy. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:scale_50clients}, \fedhep{} demonstrates robust scalability in larger edge populations, achieving $33.89\%$ mean accuracy and $18.11\%$ bottom-10\% fairness under Moderate skew (closely matching FedAvg at $33.97\%$/$20.63\%$), and $41.16\%$ mean accuracy with $16.88\%$ tail fairness under Severe skew (competitive with FedRep at $41.72\%$/$17.41\%$, while outperforming Ditto at $37.47\%$/$13.03\%$ and FedAvg at $31.87\%$/$12.81\%$).

\paragraph{Simulated On-Device Execution Footprint on MobileNetV3}
To evaluate edge feasibility, we profile simulated runtime (peak VRAM and per-batch latency via CUDA events at batch size $B=32$) on MobileNetV3-Small and ResNet-9 (Table~\ref{tab:mobilenet_edge} and Figure~\ref{fig:mobilenet_edge}). Dual-model Ditto maintains two deep graphs per client, demanding $298.60$\,MB peak VRAM and $11.36$\,ms latency on MobileNetV3-Small. In contrast, \fedhep{} trains a single backbone, reducing peak VRAM to \textbf{158.80\,MB} (a 46.8\% reduction) and batch latency to \textbf{6.29\,ms} (a 44.6\% speedup), while achieving higher CIFAR-100 accuracy (\textbf{45.43\%} vs $34.66\%$ for Ditto and $10.85\%$ for FedAvg under extreme skew).

\paragraph{Implementation Details}
We adopt a two-stage CIFAR-100 schedule (Root/Parent training, then Local-only with frozen backbone), online weight refinement, a numerical NaN/Inf guard, and TTT parameters $\beta=0.85$ (momentum decay) and $\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}=0.25$ (isolation threshold).

\paragraph{Ablation Study and Coalition Valuation}
To isolate the contribution of each architectural component, Table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} summarizes our ablation matrix across all five Dirichlet regimes on CIFAR-100 ($C=100$, ResNet-9):
(1)~\textbf{Active-Class Logit Masking (ACLM)}: Disabling ACLM (both its loss and inference masking) forces cross-entropy over unobserved classes to penalize missing labels, inducing an $-8.99$\,pp accuracy drop (from $65.49\%$ to $56.50\%$) and $-10.59$\,pp tail fairness drop under Extreme skew ($\alpha=0.05$);
(2)~\textbf{Collaborative Parent Head}: Eliminating the cluster-level Parent head reduces the architecture to a 2-tier bipartite setup ($\text{Root} + \text{Local}$), degrading extreme skew accuracy by $-5.15$\,pp (to $60.34\%$) and tail fairness by $-7.16$\,pp (to $46.76\%$), confirming that peer collaboration mitigates overfitting in isolated local heads;
(3)~\textbf{Coalition Valuation}: Enforcing a single grand coalition without clustering ($K=1$) degrades extreme skew accuracy by $-8.19$\,pp (to $57.30\%$) and tail fairness by $-8.72$\,pp, demonstrating the necessity of peer clustering under severe skew; and
(4)~\textbf{Random Projection Sketch vs. Oracle Bound}: To evaluate whether sketching compromises cluster quality, we compute the theoretical $K=3$ Oracle Bound, where clients are clustered using private label ground-truth (an ideal upper bound). Privacy-preserving Random Projection sketches ($65.49\%$) closely match this Oracle bound ($66.85\%$) under Extreme skew (a gap of only $1.36$\,pp); as Full FedHEP ($N=15$) and ablation rows ($N=10$) use different client counts, cross-row gaps are indicative.
