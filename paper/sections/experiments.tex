\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

We evaluate \fedhep{} on fine-grained vision benchmarks (FEMNIST~\cite{caldas2018leaf} and CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}) partitioned under an edge-budgeted subsampling protocol to realistically mirror on-device sample scarcity. Table~\ref{tab:exp_setup} summarizes our experimental benchmarks, Dirichlet heterogeneity regimes~\cite{hsu2019measuring}, neural backbones (ResNet-9~\cite{he2016deep} and MobileNetV3-Small~\cite{howard2019searching}), baseline paradigms~\cite{mcmahan2017communication,li2020federated,karimireddy2020scaffold,blanchard2017machine,li2021ditto,collins2021exploiting}, threat models~\cite{fang2020local}, and edge training configurations.

\input{figures/table0_setup}

All benchmark runs report mean and standard deviation across three deterministic random seeds ($\{42, 123, 7\}$). Test accuracy is evaluated on each client's private partition, while worst-decile tail fairness is quantified via the Bottom-10\% metric~\cite{li2020fair} (mean accuracy of the lowest-performing decile of edge nodes).

\subsection{Numerical Results}
\label{sec:exp_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark} and Figures~\ref{fig:femnist_personalization}--\ref{fig:cifar100_personalization} report personalization performance on FEMNIST and CIFAR-100 across five Dirichlet skew regimes. In benign IID settings, global consensus models (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD) achieve solid accuracy ($\approx 84\%$ on FEMNIST, $\approx 41\%$ on CIFAR-100), but degrade steadily as skew intensifies. In contrast, on FEMNIST, \fedhep{} achieves $94.73\%$ accuracy and $83.91\%$ bottom-10\% fairness under extreme skew ($\alpha=0.05$), outperforming FedAvg by $+16.52$\,pp and $+38.89$\,pp in accuracy and tail fairness, while matching heavy dual-model baselines.

On the CIFAR-100 benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark}B and Figure~\ref{fig:cifar100_personalization}), standard global consensus degrades precipitously under extreme skew: FedAvg drops to $36.29\%$ with tail fairness collapsing to $24.87\%$. While split-head FedRep personalizes under extreme skew ($60.97\%$), it experiences severe representation collapse on homogeneous IID data ($16.08\%$, a 25.38pp drop below FedAvg). In contrast, \fedhep{} achieves superior accuracy under extreme skew (\textbf{65.49\%}, outperforming dual-model Ditto by $+5.81\mathrm{pp}$ and FedRep by $+4.52\mathrm{pp}$) with the highest worst-decile fairness (\textbf{53.92\%}), while completely eliminating the split-head IID collapse ($40.94\%$ on IID data). Under moderate skew ($\alpha=0.5$), \fedhep{} achieves \textbf{46.36\%} personalized accuracy and $39.86\%$ tail fairness, consistently surpassing Ditto ($43.84\%$ / $37.55\%$) and FedAvg ($39.80\%$ / $35.19\%$).

Table~\ref{tab:cifar100_byzantine} and Figure~\ref{fig:byzantine_robustness} assess resilience under four coordinated adversarial vectors on CIFAR-100: Label Flipping, Sign Flipping, Adversarial Gradient Ascent, and Gaussian Noise Injection, across attacker fractions $q \in \{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$. A key empirical finding is the failure mode of classical robust aggregation under non-IID data: Multi-Krum~\cite{blanchard2017machine} collapses even in benign settings without attackers ($q=0.0$, achieving only $25.04\%$), because legitimate label divergence is conflated with poisoning. By evaluating update alignment strictly within observed label support (\sccf{}), bounding backbone gradient norms, and tracking temporal trust (\ttt{}), \fedhep{} sustains high accuracy in benign environments (\textbf{47.88\%}) and maintains robust performance up to $q=0.30$. Under Gaussian noise ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} maintains \textbf{44.53\%} accuracy (a drop of only $-3.35$\,pp), whereas FedAvg collapses to $1.30\%$ and Multi-Krum achieves $22.96\%$. Under gradient ascent ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} sustains \textbf{28.93\%} compared to $1.81\%$ for Ditto and $1.06\%$ for FedAvg.

To evaluate behavior in larger multiagent edge populations, we scale the network to $N=50$ clients with 20\% partial client participation per round ($C_p=0.20$, Table~\ref{tab:scale_50clients} and Figure~\ref{fig:scalability_50clients}). Using our personalized evaluation protocol with Synthetic Active Feature Replay (S-AFR) fallback, \fedhep{} lifts the bottom-10\% fairness floor to $18.11\%$ under Moderate skew ($\alpha=0.5$) and $16.88\%$ under Severe skew ($\alpha=0.1$). In the Severe regime, \fedhep{} achieves $41.16\%$ mean accuracy, substantially outperforming Ditto ($37.47\%$ / $13.03\%$) and FedAvg ($31.87\%$ / $12.81\%$), validating equitable convergence across scaled multiagent systems.

To evaluate edge resource feasibility without overclaiming physical microcontroller deployment, we profile simulated runtime metrics (peak VRAM via \texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated} and batch latency via CUDA events at $B=32$) on MobileNetV3-Small and ResNet-9 (Table~\ref{tab:mobilenet_edge} and Figure~\ref{fig:mobilenet_edge}). Dual-model Ditto maintains two distinct deep graphs per client, demanding $298.60$\,MB peak VRAM and $22.80$\,ms batch latency on MobileNetV3-Small. In contrast, \fedhep{} trains a single shared feature extractor, reducing peak VRAM to \textbf{158.80\,MB} (a 46.8\% reduction) and batch latency to \textbf{11.20\,ms} (a 50.9\% speedup), while achieving superior downstream personalization on CIFAR-100 (\textbf{45.43\%} vs $34.66\%$ for Ditto and $10.85\%$ for FedAvg under extreme skew).

To rigorously isolate the contribution of each architectural component, Table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} summarizes our ablation matrix across all five Dirichlet regimes on CIFAR-100 ($C=100$, ResNet-9):
(1)~\textbf{Active-Class Logit Masking (ACLM)}: Disabling ACLM forces cross-entropy over unobserved classes to penalize missing labels, inducing an acute $-8.99$\,pp accuracy drop (from $65.49\%$ to $56.50\%$) and $-10.59$\,pp tail fairness drop under Extreme skew ($\alpha=0.05$);
(2)~\textbf{Collaborative Parent Head}: Eliminating the cluster-level Parent head reduces the architecture to a 2-tier bipartite setup ($\text{Root} + \text{Local}$), degrading extreme skew accuracy by $-5.15$\,pp (to $60.34\%$) and tail fairness by $-7.16$\,pp (to $46.76\%$), proving that peer collaboration across clients with overlapping label subsets prevents isolated local heads from overfitting;
(3)~\textbf{Coalition Valuation}: Enforcing a single grand coalition without clustering ($K=1$) degrades extreme skew accuracy by $-8.19$\,pp (to $57.30\%$) and tail fairness by $-8.72$\,pp, demonstrating the necessity of specialized peer sub-coalitions under acute statistical heterogeneity; and
(4)~\textbf{Random Projection Sketch vs. Oracle Bound}: Privacy-preserving Random Projection sketches ($65.49\%$) closely track the theoretical Oracle label clustering bound ($66.85\%$) within a tight gap of only $1.36$\,pp ($98.0\%$ of oracle performance), verifying that low-dimensional functional sketches discover synergistic peer clusters without exposing private local distributions.
