\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
\paragraph{Datasets and Non-IID Partitions.} We evaluate our framework on \textbf{CIFAR-100}~\cite{kairouz2021advances}, consisting of 60,000 $32 \times 32$ images distributed across 100 fine-grained categories. To evaluate robust personalization under progressive data skew, we partition training samples across $N=10$ (and scaled to $N=50$) clients using symmetric Dirichlet distributions $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)$ across five distinct regimes: IID ($\alpha = \infty$), Mild Non-IID ($\alpha = 1.0$), Moderate Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.5$), Severe Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.1$), and Extreme Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.05$). At $\alpha = 0.05$, clients hold data from only 3--6 distinct classes, representing acute real-world edge skew.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare \hepfl{} against six prominent FL baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Consensus}: \textsc{FedAvg}~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}, \textsc{FedProx}~\cite{li2020federated} ($\mu=0.01$), and \textsc{SCAFFOLD}~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}.
    \item \textbf{Byzantine-Robust Baseline}: \textsc{Multi-Krum}~\cite{blanchard2017machine}.
    \item \textbf{Personalized Baselines}: \textsc{Ditto}~\cite{li2021ditto} (dual-model with $\lambda=0.1$) and \textsc{FedRep}~\cite{collins2021exploiting} (decoupled local heads).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Models and Hyperparameters.} For primary evaluations, all methods employ a compact ResNet-9 backbone ($d=512$). For physical edge hardware profiling, we deploy MobileNetV3-Small~\cite{howard2019searching}. Training is executed over $T=100$ communication rounds with local epochs $E=5$, batch size $B=32$, SGD with momentum $0.9$, learning rate $\eta=0.01$, weight decay $5 \times 10^{-4}$, and cosine annealing. Evaluations are conducted across 3 independent seeds ($42, 123, 7$).

\subsection{Byzantine Robustness Under Heterogeneous Skew}
\label{sec:exp_byzantine}
Table~\ref{tab:byzantine} presents test accuracy on CIFAR-100 under four adversarial attack vectors with corruption ratios $q \in [0.0, 0.4]$: label flipping, sign flipping, adversarial gradient ascent, and Gaussian noise injection. 

Standard robust aggregators like \textsc{Multi-Krum} suffer catastrophic accuracy collapse under non-IID skew even in benign settings ($q=0.0$), because legitimate angular skew is misclassified as poisoning. By evaluating update alignment strictly within observed class support (\textbf{SCCF}) and clamping backbone updates, \hepfl{} maintains robust accuracy even under 40\% active adversaries ($q=0.4$).

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
We perform ablation experiments to isolate the contribution of each architectural component:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Effect of ACLM}: Removing Active-Class Logit Masking causes backpropagation interference on the shared backbone, resulting in a 4.2\% drop in mean accuracy under $\alpha=0.1$.
    \item \textbf{Value of Parent Head}: Eliminating the collaborative Parent Head reduces performance to a purely disjoint bipartite setup, degrading collaborative representation among similar clients by 2.8\%.
    \item \textbf{Cluster Allocation}: Random projection clustering achieves 94.6\% of the accuracy attainable by a hypothetical Oracle clustering possessing ground-truth Dirichlet label distributions.
\end{itemize}
