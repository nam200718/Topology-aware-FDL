\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
\paragraph{Datasets and Heterogeneous Partitions.} We evaluate \fedhep{} across two distinct image classification benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CIFAR-100}~\cite{kairouz2021advances}: 100 fine-grained categories with 10,000 training and 3,000 test images partitioned across $N=10$ (and scaled to $N=50$) edge clients.
    \item \textbf{FEMNIST}~\cite{caldas2018leaf}: 62 upper/lowercase and digit categories with 15,000 training and 3,000 test images partitioned across $N=15$ clients.
\end{itemize}
To evaluate robust personalization under progressive data skew, we partition training samples using symmetric Dirichlet distributions $\mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)$ across five distinct regimes: IID ($\alpha = \infty$), Mild Non-IID ($\alpha = 1.0$), Moderate Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.5$), Severe Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.1$), and Extreme Non-IID ($\alpha = 0.05$). Under extreme skew ($\alpha = 0.05$), clients hold data from only 3--6 distinct categories, representing acute real-world edge heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare \fedhep{} against six prominent FL baselines spanning three representative paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Consensus}: \textsc{FedAvg}~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}, \textsc{FedProx}~\cite{li2020federated} ($\mu=0.01$), and \textsc{SCAFFOLD}~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}.
    \item \textbf{Byzantine-Robust Defense}: \textsc{Multi-Krum}~\cite{blanchard2017machine}.
    \item \textbf{Personalized Architectures}: \textsc{Ditto}~\cite{li2021ditto} (dual-model with $\lambda=0.05$) and \textsc{FedRep}~\cite{collins2021exploiting} (decoupled representation with frozen backbone).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Models, Budget FL Protocols, and Seeds.} For primary evaluations, all methods employ a compact ResNet-9 backbone ($d=512$). For edge resource profiling, we evaluate MobileNetV3-Small~\cite{howard2019searching}. Under a realistic edge budget regime, communication rounds are parameterized as $R=25$ for multi-regime benchmarks, $R=30$ for Byzantine poisoning sweeps, and $R=20$ (and $R=40$) for 50-client scalability. Training utilizes local epochs $E=3$, batch size $B=64$ (ResNet-9) / $B=32$ (MobileNetV3), SGD with momentum $0.9$, learning rate $\eta \in [0.02, 0.05]$, and weight decay $5 \times 10^{-4}$. All benchmarks report Mean $\pm$ Standard Deviation across three independent random seeds ($42, 123, 7$).

As statistical skew intensifies from IID to Extreme ($\alpha=0.05$), standard consensus models (\textsc{FedAvg}, \textsc{FedProx}) degrade substantially due to acute client drift, dropping to 36.29\% on CIFAR-100. \subsection{Byzantine Robustness Under Heterogeneous Skew}
\label{sec:exp_byzantine}
Table~\ref{tab:cifar100_byzantine} presents CIFAR-100 test accuracy under four adversarial model-poisoning vectors with corruption fractions $q \in [0.0, 0.4]$: label flipping, sign flipping, adversarial gradient ascent, and Gaussian noise injection.

\input{figures/table3_byzantine}
\FloatBarrier

Conventional robust aggregators like \textsc{Multi-Krum} suffer catastrophic degradation under statistical skew even in benign settings ($q=0.0$, achieving only $25.04\%$), because legitimate angular gradients from specialized clients are misdiagnosed as malicious poisoning. Under moderate corruption ($q \le 0.30$), Defended \fedhep{} demonstrates superior resilience: under Gaussian noise ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} maintains $44.53\%$ accuracy (a drop of only $-3.35$\,pp from $47.88\%$), whereas \textsc{FedAvg} collapses to $1.30\%$ and \textsc{Multi-Krum} achieves $22.96\%$. Under gradient ascent ($q=0.3$), \fedhep{} sustains $28.93\%$ compared to $1.81\%$ for \textsc{Ditto} and $1.06\%$ for \textsc{FedAvg}. When corruption approaches the theoretical multi-round breakdown boundary ($q=0.40$), shared backbone updates incur degradation across all methods, delineating $q \le 0.30$ as the reliable operational threshold under extreme non-IID conditions.

Under partial participation where clients participate only in a fraction of rounds, unparticipating edge nodes rely on robust personalized heads. \subsection{Ablation Study and Coalition Valuation}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_study} isolates the contribution of each architectural tier and algorithmic component across all five Dirichlet regimes on CIFAR-100:

\input{figures/table6_ablation}
\FloatBarrier

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Isolation of ACLM}: Removing Active-Class Logit Masking causes false negative gradient backpropagation from unobserved categories onto the shared backbone, resulting in a steep $-8.99$\,pp accuracy drop (from $65.49\%$ to $56.50\%$) and a $-10.59$\,pp tail fairness drop under Extreme skew ($\alpha=0.05$).
    \item \textbf{Value of the Collaborative Parent Head}: Eliminating the collaborative middle tier reduces the architecture to a 2-tier bipartite setup, degrading extreme skew accuracy by $-5.15$\,pp (to $60.34\%$) and tail fairness by $-7.16$\,pp (to $46.76\%$), proving that peer collaboration prevents isolated local heads from overfitting.
    \item \textbf{Grand Coalition ($K=1$) vs. Clustering}: Enforcing a single global coalition ($K=1$) degrades extreme skew accuracy by $-8.19$\,pp (to $57.30\%$) and tail fairness by $-8.72$\,pp, demonstrating the necessity of peer sub-coalitions under severe non-IID drift.
    \item \textbf{Random Projection Sketch vs. Oracle Clustering Bound}: Comparing privacy-preserving Random Projection clustering ($65.49\%$) against a theoretical Oracle clustering possessing ground-truth label distributions ($66.85\%$) reveals a tight gap of only $1.36$\,pp ($98.0\%$ of oracle performance), demonstrating that low-dimensional functional sketches discover synergistic peer clusters without raw gradient exposure.
\end{itemize}
