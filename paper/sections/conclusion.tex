\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we addressed the fundamental trilemma of federated learning in resource-constrained multiagent edge topologies: client drift under non-IID statistical heterogeneity, memory and computational bloat in personalized models, and Byzantine vulnerability under legitimate data skew. We proposed \textbf{\hepfl{}}, a unified single-backbone framework coordinating three representation tiers: global consensus, collaborative cluster, and local private heads. 

By incorporating Active-Class Logit Masking (\textbf{ACLM}) and skew-calibrated loss weighting, \hepfl{} eliminates negative gradient interference without duplicating deep networks. Privacy-preserving Random Projections (\textbf{RP}) enable clients to self-organize into synergistic peer clusters, while Subspace-Constrained Cosine Filtering (\textbf{SCCF}), adaptive norm-bounding, and Temporal Trust Tracking (\textbf{TTT}) protect the network against sophisticated Byzantine attacks without penalizing honest specialized clients. Extensive empirical evaluations across five non-IID regimes on CIFAR-100 and real-world edge hardware profiling on MobileNetV3 confirm that \hepfl{} achieves state-of-the-art personalization accuracy and worst-decile tail fairness while preserving the lean runtime efficiency of single-model architectures. Future research will explore formal differential privacy guarantees for parameter sketches and extend the hierarchical ensemble paradigm to asynchronous multi-tier heterogeneous edge devices.
