\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we addressed three central challenges of federated learning on resource-constrained edge devices: client drift under non-IID data distributions, high memory overhead in personalized dual-model architectures, and false-positive outlier filtering under Byzantine attacks. We presented \textbf{\fedhep{}}, a single-backbone framework coordinating three representation tiers: global consensus, collaborative peer clusters, and private local heads.

By incorporating Active-Class Logit Masking (\textbf{ACLM}) and skew-calibrated loss weighting, \fedhep{} eliminates gradient interference from missing labels without duplicating deep networks. Privacy-preserving Random Projections enable clients to identify synergistic peer clusters without sharing raw model weights, while Subspace-Constrained Cosine Filtering (\textbf{SCCF}), adaptive norm-bounding, and Temporal Trust Tracking (\textbf{TTT}) protect the network against Byzantine poisoning without penalizing honest specialized clients. Extensive empirical evaluations across five non-IID regimes on CIFAR-100 and edge runtime profiling on MobileNetV3 confirm that \fedhep{} achieves strong local accuracy and tail fairness while preserving the memory and latency efficiency of single-model architectures. Future work will investigate formal differential privacy guarantees for parameter sketches and extend the hierarchical framework to asynchronous client participation.
